\section{Problem Model}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Program and release.}
A black-box program $P$ maps records $R$, reference $E$, and options $o$ to a
tabular body (headers ignored). Each entry of the ordered list $E$ has a strict
hash $h(e)$ (SHA-256 after dropping caller-declared volatile tags). A release
$E\!\to\!E'$ yields stable $S=\{e\in E':h(e)\in h(E)\}$, changed
$\Delta=E'\setminus S$, and $f_\Delta=|\Delta|/|E'|$.
% source: MEASURED pipeline/results/reference_kill_r1.json unchanged_strict=26082 z_new=30134; DERIVED identical_pct=100*unchanged_strict/z_new; NOMINAL sha_bits=256
For each pair $(r,e)$, let $f(r,e,o,g(E'))$ be its possibly empty sequence
of emitted body rows, including bytes and multiplicities, where $g(E')$
collects reference-wide quantities (entry count, length, \ldots).
Exact re-annotation reproduces $\mathrm{body}(P(R,E',o))$ under the
conditions below; an observed check failure returns the full run instead.

\paragraph{Coupling taxonomy.}
\textbf{C0} (entry-local): $f$ independent of $g$ and of every $e'\neq e$
(Infernal's no-clan variant) $\rightarrow$ replay.
\textbf{C1} (reference-size): $f$ depends on $E'$ only through a normalizer in
$g$ that $P$ exposes or \sys rescales (HMMER \texttt{-Z}) $\rightarrow$ pin, or
refuse if uncovered.
\textbf{C2} (neighbor/order): the line for $(r,e)$ depends on neighbors of $e$
or on input order (DIAMOND) $\rightarrow$ refuse.
\textbf{C3} (cross-record global, e.g.\ FDR) and \textbf{C4} (nondeterministic)
$\rightarrow$ refuse when observed.
Refusal rules R1--R6 form one surface: R1 no contract; R2 size-dependent, no
pin; R3 in-situ pin check fails; R4 non-candidate pair in replay; R5 pad changes
a line; R6 audit differs. C0/C1 replay; C2--C4 refuse (R1--R3 on the probe,
R4--R6 on the update). Side-file rewrites and invalidate-groups are
\textit{declarations}; they do not establish entry locality for competing groups.

\paragraph{Exactness (C0/C1).}
\label{asm:det}\label{asm:pin}\label{asm:key}\label{asm:order}%
Assumptions: (D)~\textbf{determinism}---fixed $(r,e,o,g)$ emits the same rows;
(P)~\textbf{complete pin}---pinned options fix every reference-wide quantity
affecting row existence, multiplicity, or bytes to its full-release value,
including quantities not observed by the fitter, without changing other
output-relevant behavior;
(K)~\textbf{attribution}---each row occurrence is attributable to its record
and entry, preserving repeated occurrences;
(O)~\textbf{exact assembly}---the merge reconstructs stock ordering and
formatting from these rows and the original record/reference order;
(C)~\textbf{candidate completeness}---every entry producing a full-release
row for $r$ belongs to its candidate set $C_r$;
(I)~\textbf{record-subset invariance}---with reference and options fixed,
running on a subset of records produces exactly the projection of the
full-record body onto that subset.
\begin{proposition}[Exact merge]\label{prop:exact}
Assume C0 or C1 and (D), (P), (K), (O), (C), and (I).
Running $P$ on each $r$ against $C_r$ with $g$ pinned, then applying
the merge in (O), yields $\mathrm{body}(P(R,E',o))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By (I), isolating $r$ preserves its full-reference rows.
Under C0/C1 and (P), restricting the reference to $C_r$ preserves the
emitted rows for each retained pair, including empty sequences and
multiplicities; (D) excludes execution drift. By (C), every omitted pair
emits no full-release row. Replay therefore produces exactly the
full-release row occurrences; (K) preserves attribution and (O)
reconstructs the full body.
\end{proof}
The probe (R1), pin check (R3), pads (R5), and non-candidate tripwire (R4) test
these assumptions; they do not prove C0/C1 on the full input. Not covered:
C2--C4; residual couplings below $\kappa$ or that evade pads and the audit;
limitation F6 (coupling outside the closed family that still looks locally
consistent).

\paragraph{Audit bound.}
Let $A$ be the stated audit population and fix the affected records
independently of the audit draw. Under (I), sample-only stock is a valid
oracle. If at least a fraction $\kappa$ of $A$ differs, a uniform sample
of $s\le |A|$ records without replacement misses them with probability
at most $(1-\kappa)^s$. The conservative with-replacement sizing rule is
\begin{equation}
s=\bigl\lceil\ln\varepsilon/\ln(1-\kappa)\bigr\rceil.
\label{eq:audit-s}
\end{equation}
% source: MEASURED pipeline/docs/EXACT_REPLAY_PROTOCOL.md; TEXT s=299; NOMINAL eps=0.05; NOMINAL kappa=0.01; NOMINAL one=1
For $\varepsilon=0.05$, $\kappa=0.01$, $s=299$; if $|A|<s$, audit all of $A$.
The bound requires that the stated audit executes with a valid oracle.
For the historical bounded timings, $A$ contains only candidate-bearing
records; the all-record policy uses $A=R$ and requires its own timing evidence.
In the historical adversarial evaluation, 3 of 400 trials shipped when
at least 1\% of its audit population was affected.
% source: MEASURED pipeline/results/adversarial/v2_summary.json arms.bounded.covered_unsafe=3 arms.bounded.covered_trials=400; NOMINAL eps=0.05; NOMINAL kappa=0.01; NOMINAL s=299; NOMINAL one=1
\begin{plainbox}{What the audit does \emph{not} cover}
It is not a 5\% per\nobreakdash-line error rate, nor a proof that the program is entry\nobreakdash-local.
Couplings that affect fewer than $\kappa$ of records can still ship; so can structured faults that evade the pads and the sample (limitation F6).
C2--C4 are refused when observed (R1--R6), not ``covered'' by $s{=}299$.
\end{plainbox}

\paragraph{Cost model.}
With stock time $T_{\mathrm{stock}}$,
\begin{equation}
T_{\sys}\approx a\,f_\Delta\,T_{\mathrm{stock}}+c\,T_{\mathrm{stock}},
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $a$ scales selection and $c$ collects replay, check, audit, and parsing.
Fallback when $f_\Delta>(1-c)/a$; default threshold $0.80$ with no timing history.
% source: MEASURED pipeline/results/breakeven/measured.json threshold_default=0.8; NOMINAL one=1
Programs whose rows factor as $f(r,e,o,g(E))$ and satisfy the exact-merge
assumptions fit; C2--C4 remain refuse
classes. \sys never reads the program's source at run time.
